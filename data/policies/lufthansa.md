# LUFTHANSA BOOKING POLICY DOCUMENT  
## Structured for GraphRAG Dissertation Testing

---

## **LH1: General Booking Terms**

All Lufthansa bookings are subject to fare rules applicable at time of purchase.

---

## **LH2: Economy Light Fare**

Economy Light is the most restrictive economy fare and is generally non-refundable after the applicable free-cancellation period. Checked baggage is usually not included; the fare is designed around hand baggage only, with paid options for checked bags and extras. Changes to travel dates are permitted only with payment of rebooking fees plus any fare difference, and the no-show rules may lead to loss of ticket value if you do not travel as booked.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH15, LH19, LH28, LH29

---

## **LH3: Economy Classic Fare**

Economy Classic offers more flexibility than Light, typically including at least one piece of checked baggage and free standard seat reservation on many routes. Changes are allowed subject to the published timing-based rebooking fees, route-based supplements, and any fare difference between the old and new itinerary. Classic fares may carry minimum-stay or advance-purchase conditions, and missing a segment can trigger re-rating or cancellation of remaining flights.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH15, LH19, LH20, LH23, LH28, LH29, LH30

---

## **LH4: Economy Flex Fare**

Economy Flex provides the highest flexibility within economy, often allowing rebooking with reduced or waived change fees compared with Light and Classic. Refunds may be permitted according to the fare rules, with unused portions subject to recalculation under partial-journey principles. Flex customers can change travel plans with more favourable conditions if flight times change involuntarily or if they choose to alter segments.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH18, LH23, LH24, LH28

---

## **LH5: 24‑Hour Free Cancellation**

Lufthansa allows risk-free cancellation within a defined period (commonly 24 hours) for tickets booked directly, provided certain timing conditions around departure are met. Within this window, eligible bookings can be cancelled online for a full refund without standard change or cancellation fees; after the window expires, normal fare conditions apply. Customers can sometimes choose between a cash refund and flight credit options, with subsequent processing following the usual refund and credit rules.

**References:** LH21, LH24

---

## **LH6: Premium Economy Basic Fare**

Premium Economy Basic provides a wider seat and enhanced comfort compared with economy, generally including two checked bags and upgraded in-flight services. Changes are allowed with timing-based rebooking fees and route-based supplements, and refunds may be limited or subject to penalties, depending on the sub-fare. In cases of operational downgrades, Premium Economy Basic passengers may be moved to Economy and become eligible for downgrade compensation.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH15, LH22, LH28, LH29

---

## **LH7: Premium Economy Flex Fare**

Premium Economy Flex offers the most flexible conditions in Premium Economy, often with lower or no change fees compared with Basic, and better refund options. Passengers benefit from two checked bags, enhanced seating, and more favourable handling when flights are changed involuntarily or cancelled. If rerouting or schedule changes occur, Premium Economy Flex customers can often rebook without additional rebooking charges, subject to availability and fare difference.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH18, LH22, LH24, LH28

---

## **LH8: Business Saver Fare**

Business Saver gives access to Business Class service at discounted conditions, usually with some restrictions on flexibility. It typically includes two checked bags at higher weight limits, lounge access, and Business Class in-flight services, but changes and refunds are more restricted than fully flexible business fares. Saver fares may include minimum-stay or advance-purchase rules and charge change fees plus route-based supplements if travel plans are altered.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH15, LH20, LH22, LH28, LH29

---

## **LH9: Business Flex Fare**

Business Flex provides full Business Class benefits with maximum flexibility, often allowing changes close to departure with minimal or no rebooking fee. Tickets are generally refundable under favourable conditions, and customers receive priority when flights are disrupted or schedules change. Business Flex passengers are also covered by the same downgrading and compensation rules if they are moved to a lower cabin due to operational reasons.

**References:** LH5, LH14, LH18, LH22, LH24, LH28, LH29

---

## **LH10: First Class Flex Fare**

First Class Flex offers the highest level of service, flexibility, and refundability in the Lufthansa network. Passengers benefit from generous baggage allowances, premium ground services, and flexible rebooking and refund options defined by the fare conditions. In rare cases of downgrades or schedule disruption, First Class travellers have strong rights to compensation, rerouting, or refunds consistent with EU passenger rights and internal carrier rules.

**References:** LH5, LH18, LH22, LH24, LH28, LH29

---

## **LH11: Same‑Day Standby & Earlier Flight Options**

Lufthansa may allow same-day changes to earlier or later flights on the same route and day, depending on the fare type and seat availability. These options are usually more favourable for flexible fares, while restrictive fares may either prohibit same-day moves or charge rebooking fees and any fare difference. The cost and feasibility are tied to the general timing-based change rules, route supplements, and whether any residual value or fees need refund handling.

**References:** LH14, LH15, LH24

---

## **LH12: Miles & More Earning by Fare Type**

Miles & More mileage accrual varies by booking class and fare family, with Economy Light typically earning the lowest percentage and Business/First Flex earning the highest. Flex and premium fares may earn higher mileage, while discounted fares can earn reduced percentages according to the current earning tables. Accrued miles can later be redeemed following award ticket and voucher usage rules, linking fare choice to loyalty benefits.

**References:** LH13, LH21

---

## **LH13: Miles & More Award Ticket Rules**

Award tickets booked with Miles & More miles follow specific rules around changes, cancellations, and mileage redeposit. Some award types allow changes or cancellations with a mileage or cash fee, while others may be more restrictive, and taxes/surcharges are usually treated separately from miles. If an award is cancelled or rebooked, any refund of miles and money follows the general voucher and refund-processing timelines used by Lufthansa and Miles & More.

**References:** LH21, LH24

---

## **LH14: Timing‑Based Rebooking & Change Fees**

Lufthansa applies different rebooking fees depending on how far in advance changes are made, with higher fees closer to departure. These fees are charged per passenger and direction, in addition to fare differences if the new flight is more expensive than the original. Timing-based fees interact with route-based supplements and segment-level re-rating rules, and they can also influence the calculations used during refund processing.

**References:** LH15, LH23, LH24

---

## **LH15: Route‑Based Surcharges for Changes**

Change and rebooking surcharges can differ between short-haul European flights and long-haul intercontinental routes. Intercontinental or premium routes often carry higher supplements for changes than short-haul flights, reflecting the underlying fare and cost structure. These surcharges are added on top of timing-based rebooking fees and are also considered when recalculating fares for partial journeys or rerated itineraries.

**References:** LH23

---

## **LH16: Lufthansa‑Initiated Flight Cancellations**

When Lufthansa cancels a flight, passengers are entitled to a choice between rerouting at the earliest opportunity or a refund of the unused ticket portion. The refund includes the fare and taxes for the unused segments, and in some cases, passengers may choose vouchers or credits as an alternative. For flights covered by EU passenger rights, additional compensation may be owed if the cancellation is not caused by extraordinary circumstances and notice thresholds are not met.

**References:** LH17, LH21, LH24, LH25, LH26

---

## **LH17: Flight Delays & Duty of Care**

In the event of significant delays, Lufthansa must provide care such as meals, refreshments, and hotel accommodation where necessary, especially for overnight disruptions. The thresholds for care depend on route length and delay duration, and passengers may still have EU compensation rights if the cause is within the airline’s control and the delay meets the time criteria. Travel credits or vouchers can sometimes be offered as a gesture of goodwill or in line with disruption policies, in addition to formal rights.

**References:** LH21, LH25, LH26

---

## **LH18: Involuntary Schedule Changes & Rerouting**

Schedule changes may alter departure or arrival times or flight numbers and can give passengers the option to accept the new schedule, rebook, or request refunds, depending on severity. For substantial changes, Lufthansa may allow free rebooking within certain parameters or refunds if the new timings are not acceptable. On routes subject to European passenger rights, a significant schedule change may be treated similarly to cancellation for compensation purposes, and claim procedures are set out in passenger rights information.

**References:** LH21, LH24, LH25, LH26

---

## **LH19: No‑Show, Late Check‑In & Segment Use Rules**

If a passenger fails to show up for a flight or misses check-in deadlines, Lufthansa may mark the ticket as a no-show and cancel subsequent segments. No-shows on restrictive fares like Economy Light can lead to loss of ticket value, while more flexible fares might still be reissued subject to fees and recalculation. The impact on fare and any potential residual value is governed by partial-journey re-rating rules and the general refund provisions.

**References:** LH23, LH24

---

## **LH20: Minimum/Maximum Stay & Advance Purchase Conditions**

Some discounted fares, particularly in Economy Classic and Business Saver, require a minimum stay at destination (such as a Saturday night) or enforce maximum stays and advance-purchase limits. Violating these conditions by returning earlier or staying longer than allowed can trigger a fare recalculation to a higher applicable fare. When such changes occur, the new total price is determined under the partial-journey and re-rating rules, adding change fees and any fare difference.

**References:** LH23

---

## **LH21: Flight Credit, E‑Voucher & Rebooking Credits**

Lufthansa may issue flight credits or e‑vouchers in cases like disruptions, cancellations, or voluntary changes chosen by the customer. These credits usually have a defined validity period and can be applied toward new tickets, including higher cabins or different routes, with any fare difference to be paid by the customer. The actual credit issuance and usage follow the general refund and accounting timelines, and any issues or exceptions are handled through customer service channels.

**References:** LH24, LH32

---

## **LH22: Involuntary Cabin Downgrade Compensation**

If Lufthansa assigns a customer to a lower cabin than originally booked for operational reasons, the passenger is entitled to compensation based on a percentage of the ticket price for the affected sector. The percentage depends on flight distance and is aligned with relevant passenger rights where applicable, especially for flights departing from or arriving in the EU on an EU carrier. Passengers must usually claim downgrade compensation through the airline’s compensation or passenger rights channels, referencing their disrupted itinerary.

**References:** LH25, LH26

---

## **LH23: Partial Journey Cancellations & Rerating**

When only part of an itinerary is changed or cancelled, Lufthansa recalculates the fare using re-rating rules for the flown and remaining sectors. This may result in an additional amount to be paid or a partial refund, depending on whether the new combination is priced higher or lower than the original. Any applicable timing-based change fees, route-based surcharges, and no-show effects are included in the recalculation, and resulting refunds or charges are then processed under the general rules.

**References:** LH24

---

## **LH24: Refund Processing & Ticket Validity**

Refunds are processed according to Lufthansa’s refund timelines, which can vary depending on payment method and location, typically taking several working days to reach the customer after approval. Ticket validity is usually limited to a fixed period, and unused tickets or portions may be refundable only within that period and under the fare rules. Refund and voucher-related queries, including escalations, are handled via passenger-rights information channels and customer service teams.

**References:** LH26, LH32

---

## **LH25: EU261 / Passenger Rights Compensation**

Under European passenger rights regulations, compensation may be due for cancellations, long delays, and denied boarding on eligible flights. The standard compensation levels are typically distance-based amounts per passenger, and may be reduced if re-routing results in limited arrival delay. Compensation is not owed when disruptions are caused by extraordinary circumstances, such as severe weather or air traffic control strikes, but duty of care often still applies.

**References:** LH26, LH32

---

## **LH26: Passenger Rights Information & Claim Channels**

Lufthansa provides detailed information on passenger rights and claim procedures on its website and via dedicated customer service units. Passengers can submit claims for compensation, refunds, downgrade compensation, and other issues through online forms or written correspondence, supplying evidence such as booking references and disruption details. Escalation paths may include regulatory bodies or alternative dispute resolution where applicable, with customer service acting as the first point of contact.

**References:** LH32

---

## **LH27: Involuntary & Voluntary Denied Boarding**

If a flight is overbooked, Lufthansa first seeks volunteers willing to give up their seats in exchange for negotiated benefits such as vouchers or rebooking. Passengers denied boarding against their will are entitled to compensation, care (meals, accommodation if needed), and a choice between refund and rerouting. Claims for denied boarding compensation and related expenses are filed through the airline’s passenger rights and customer service channels.

**References:** LH25, LH26, LH32

---

## **LH28: Special Seat Reservations (Extra Legroom, Preferred Zone)**

Lufthansa sells reserved seats such as extra-legroom or preferred-zone seats, with availability and pricing varying by route, cabin, and fare type. Economy Light often has no free advance seat reservation, while Classic and higher fares may include free standard seat selection, with special seats offered at an extra charge. Requests, changes, or issues with special seat reservations are handled via online self-service tools or customer service channels.

**References:** LH32

---

## **LH29: Sports Equipment, Musical Instruments & Special Baggage**

Sports equipment and large musical instruments are treated as special baggage and may require advance registration and additional charges depending on size, weight, and routing. Some items can be counted within the standard baggage allowance, while others are charged separately according to Lufthansa’s special baggage tables. Questions about acceptance, fees, and handling are managed through baggage information pages and customer service prior to departure.

**References:** LH32

---

## **LH30: Children, Infants & Family Seating Rules**

Infants typically travel on an adult’s lap with reduced-price infant tickets, while children require their own seat and age-appropriate fare. Lufthansa aims to seat children close to their accompanying adults, and certain family seating practices or free seat assignments may be applied to keep families together when possible. When children travel alone, different rules apply under the unaccompanied minor program, and parents may need to coordinate with customer service to ensure proper handling.

**References:** LH31, LH32

---

## **LH31: Unaccompanied Minors (UM)**

Lufthansa’s unaccompanied minor service applies to children travelling without an adult, with age thresholds and mandatory UM booking in specific ranges. Parents or guardians must book the service in advance, complete UM forms, and provide contact information for both departure and arrival points. During disruptions, Lufthansa coordinates care, rebooking, and communication through its UM procedures and customer service channels to keep guardians informed.

**References:** LH32

---

## **LH32: Customer Service & Digital Self‑Service Channels**

Lufthansa offers multiple service channels, including website, mobile app, phone centres, and airport desks, for managing bookings, changes, refunds, and claims. Many changes and cancellations can be done online, but complex cases such as re-rating, compensation, or special baggage often require direct contact with customer service. Contact details and links for passenger rights, complaints, and special service requests are published on the airline’s official customer support and passenger rights pages.
